1The starting point

Cities are asked to deliver the future — but rarely allowed to imagine it.

The biggest constraint in urban transformation isn’t technical. It’s institutional: cities are handed ever-larger mandates to act on the future, and almost none to picture it first.

2The pressure

Cities have to change fast — the pressure has never been higher.

Climate adaptation, social cohesion, economic resilience — met with a decade of stacking frameworks, from the Paris Agreement to the EU Green Deal. Cities have answered at a scale that didn’t exist ten years ago, and participation itself is now a legal duty across Europe.

National adaptation strategies (0–35)Covenant of Mayors signatories on adaptation (0–5,000)

A decade of escalating commitments, 2013–2023. National adaptation strategies rose from 20 to 32; Covenant of Mayors signatories from zero to over 4,500. The mandate to deliver the future keeps growing; the mandate to imagine it does not. · Source: European Environment Agency.

3Why it’s hard

Yet the room to imagine — and to act together — keeps shrinking.

Three institutional fractures block the very change cities are asked to deliver.

Fracture 01

No mandate to imagine

Planning law is built to administer — zone, permit, archive — never to picture and choose between futures. Participation arrives late and narrow.

Fracture 02

No capacity to match the urgency

Overloaded, rule-bound offices and thinning planner numbers leave no room to think forward.

Fracture 03

A fraying common ground

Algorithmic silos and eroding trust outside; departmental silos and tightening budgets within.

A mandate that is missing, an urgency that is mounting, a unity that is fraying. This is the gap CoPlanAI is built for.

5Definition

What CoPlanAI is — and is not.

✓What it is

  • ✓A participatory-planning platform — it structures how many people propose, compare, deliberate and converge on futures for a real place, in the room and online.
  • ✓A shared visual language — a resident’s phrase becomes an image in seconds, so expertise is no longer the price of entry.
  • ✓A way to produce a mandate — documented evidence of what a community can agree to want.

✗What it is not

  • ✗Not an urban-design tool. No masterplans, no CAD, no technical drawings. The images are scenarios to think with — indicative, never construction-ready.
  • ✗Not a render service. If the decision is already made, we’re the wrong tool.
  • ✗Not automation of the public. AI never holds the pen alone; every generative step is wrapped in a human step.
If you keep one distinction

CoPlanAI produces mandates, not masterplans. The masterplan stays your job — now with evidence of what people actually want behind it.

9Ethics

The Hippocratic Oath of working with AI.

Bringing AI into a planning organisation is not a tooling decision — it transforms the institution. So the institution owes itself an explicit ethic. Ours, in four commitments.

01

Augment judgment — never automate it away

Agents cluster, theme and summarise. Officials review the synthesis instead of compiling it — the clerical work goes, the judgment stays.

02

Preserve the right to know and to change

Every participant can see consequences and revise their choice in public — the loop the whole method is built on.

03

Make the model’s biases legible

Before asking anyone to trust its images, we show where the model leans — and let the room correct it.

04

Represent those who cannot be in the room

Children, future residents, ecosystems — voiced by tuned agents, never confused with the living public.

A working draft — we ask every partner organisation to rewrite it as their own.
10Repair

A reparative technology.

The deepest repair is to participation itself — which has a default participant (adult, articulate, free on a weekday evening) while everyone else is structurally absent. Our agents can be tuned to centre the people, and the species, planning routinely overlooks.

11Regulation & quality

Imagination, evaluated against policy.

The newest layer answers the question every authority asks next: fine, but is it compliant — and is it good? Agents score co-designed scenarios against EU policy, local design guides and municipal strategy, while professionals keep the final word.
